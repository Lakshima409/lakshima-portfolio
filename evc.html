StarView – User Experience & Interface Design

Project Overview

StarView is a conceptual cloud-based video streaming application designed for E-Video Cloud (EVC), a Sri Lankan technology start-up developing an online video platform.

The project focused on designing a user-centred digital experience rather than simply creating a collection of attractive screens. The design process explored how users discover video content, manage their personal library, interact with video players, customise their experience, and engage with features such as playlists, ratings, gifting, renting, and purchasing content.

The project was developed as part of Unit 36: User Experience and Interface Design, with the role of an Associate User Experience Engineer responsible for researching users, planning the experience, designing the interface, developing an interactive prototype, conducting user testing, analysing feedback, and iterating the design.

The Challenge

The main challenge was to design an online video platform that could support a wide range of user needs while keeping the experience simple, intuitive, engaging, and accessible.

The platform needed to support several complex activities including discovering videos and artists, renting and owning content, managing films and playlists, previewing and rating content, gifting videos, and managing payments and devices. The interface needed to provide a clear information structure and navigation system while reducing unnecessary cognitive effort for users.

My Role

Role: UX/UI Designer / Associate User Experience Engineer

Responsibilities included conducting UX research, analysing user requirements, identifying target users, developing personas and empathy maps, creating journey maps and service blueprints, designing wireframes and interactive prototypes, conducting user testing, collecting feedback, analysing responses, identifying usability problems, and iterating the design through multiple cycles.

Design Process

01 — Research & User-Centered Design

The project began by understanding the difference between User Interface (UI) and User Experience (UX). Research examined different interface types including GUIs, CLIs, touch interfaces, and voice interfaces. StarView required a Graphical User Interface (GUI) because the platform depends heavily on visual content discovery, thumbnails, navigation controls, and interactive elements.

02 — Understanding the Users

The design process focused on understanding the people who would use StarView. The selected design direction targeted younger users within approximately the 15–45 age range, with design considerations centred around technology familiarity, content discovery, customisation, social interaction, and accessibility.

03 — User Persona

A primary persona named Priya was developed to represent the target audience. The persona represented a digitally comfortable user who expects fast navigation, easy content discovery, personalised recommendations, convenient video management, and customisable settings.

04 — Empathy & Journey Mapping

Empathy maps were created to understand the user's emotional and behavioural perspective considering what they say, think, feel, and do. Customer journey maps visualised the experience across stages: Discover → Search → Select → Preview → Rent/Own → Watch → Organise → Share → Return.

05 — Information Architecture & User Flows

The interface was structured around major user activities including Home, Discovery, My Library, eM Player, Settings, and EPN (producer network). The information architecture kept frequently required functions accessible while separating discovery, consumption, organisation, and account management.

06 — Sketches & Wireframes

The design process began with sketches to explore layouts and interaction possibilities. Wireframes translated concepts into clearer interface structures, investigating navigation, screen hierarchy, content placement, search functionality, and library organisation.

07 — Visual Design

The StarView visual direction was designed around a dark interface using combinations of blue, dark blue, and dark grey. The dark theme supported the entertainment-focused nature and provided comfortable viewing in low-light environments while communicating a modern streaming-platform identity.

08 — Prototyping with Figma

Figma was selected as the primary UI/UX design tool because it supported wireframing, high-fidelity design, interactive prototyping, reusable components, and cloud-based collaboration. The prototype transformed the conceptual information architecture into an interactive interface covering major user journeys including login, home, discovery, search, video player, playlists, library, ratings, gifting, payments, and settings.

09 — User Testing & Feedback

User testing determined whether the interface satisfied user expectations. Testing focused on navigation, search, content discovery, library accessibility, player interaction, playlist management, and payment interaction. Google Forms was used to collect structured feedback through an iterative cycle: Prototype → Test → Feedback → Analyse → Redesign → Test Again.

Design Iterations

First Iteration

Key issues identified included difficult navigation within Discovery, difficulty searching for artists, Settings issues, and improvements needed for the eM Player. The Discovery page was redesigned and an additional Artist page was introduced to make artist discovery easier.

Second Iteration

Results showed strong positive responses: 80% very satisfied and 20% satisfied with search functionality, 90% satisfied with payment functionality, and 90% satisfied with search options and filters.

Third Iteration

The third iteration continued refining based on user feedback. Users described the redesigned application using positive terms such as "very user friendly," "very affordable," "nice experience," and "great app." Continued feedback revealed additional opportunities for refinement including visual customisation requests.

Key Design Decisions

  • Dark Visual Theme – Selected to suit the entertainment context
  • Dedicated Discovery Experience – Created to improve content exploration
  • Artist Page – Introduced after feedback indicated difficulty finding artists
  • Improved Search and Filters – Refined to make content discovery faster
  • Simplified Playlist Interaction – Reduced unnecessary steps
  • Improved Payment Confirmation – Made payment interactions clearer
  • Enhanced eM Player – Improved playback controls and related-content discovery
  • Cleaner Login Screen – Removed unnecessary text for focused entry point

Final Outcomes

The final StarView concept demonstrates a complete user-centred design process from research through testing and iteration. The design showed strong positive user responses:

  • 90% satisfaction with payment functionality
  • 90% satisfaction with search options and filters
  • 90% satisfaction with variety and quality of videos
  • 70% indicated they were very likely or likely to recommend the platform

Key Features Designed

  • Video Discovery and Search
  • Artist, Director, and Producer Discovery
  • My Library and Video Preview
  • Video Rental and Ownership
  • Playlist Management and Gifting
  • eM Player with Ratings and Full-Screen Playback
  • Payment Management
  • Profile Settings and Language Preferences
  • Connected Device Management
  • E-Video Producer Network

What I Learned

  • User Research Matters – Design decisions become stronger when supported by actual user requirements
  • Personas Give Direction – A persona transforms abstract research into realistic goals
  • Mapping Reveals Problems – Journey maps and UX maps provide different perspectives on experience
  • Prototypes Are Learning Tools – Value comes from testing assumptions before development
  • Feedback Must Influence Design – Testing becomes valuable when feedback converts to improvements
  • Iteration Improves UX – Repeatedly testing and refining strengthens the interface
  • Visual Design and UX Work Together – Attractive interfaces must be paired with usability
  • Figma Supports Rapid Iteration – Efficient movement between sketches, layouts, and prototypes
